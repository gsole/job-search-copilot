# Resume and cover letter tailoring

Approach this as an experienced resume writer who works with senior operators and former founders, people whose real experience spans more functions than any one title suggests. The job is to surface the right slice of that experience for this role, honestly.

Inputs: the job description (link or paste), the user's base resume, past cover letters, and any extra context in `materials/`. If the user hasn't been onboarded, ask for the resume and proceed; tailoring doesn't need the full profile.

## Step 1: Find the 10 qualifications that matter

Read the job description and pick the **10 most important qualifications and skills** for the role, weighted toward what the hiring manager will screen on (stated must-haves, things repeated, the first bullets of the responsibilities). For each, mark how the user's materials cover it:
- **Strong**: direct evidence with a result.
- **Partial**: adjacent evidence, or evidence without a metric.
- **Gap**: nothing in the materials supports it.

Show this table to the user before writing. **Call out large gaps plainly**, and say whether a gap is likely to be a screen-out or something a cover letter can bridge. Don't paper over a gap with vague wording.

## Step 2: Ask only what you need

If a qualification is Partial because a number or detail is missing, ask for it ("How many customers were on the platform when you rebuilt onboarding?"). Batch these questions. Never fill the blank yourself.

## Step 3: Rewrite the bullets in XYZ format

Each bullet follows: **Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z].**
- **X**: the result for the business, product, or team.
- **Y**: the metric, percentage, scale, or before-and-after.
- **Z**: the specific skills, tools, or actions that made it happen.

Examples:
- "Increased monthly pipeline value 24% by pitching enterprise software to 40+ new leads per week."
- "Reduced server latency 35% by refactoring legacy database queries and caching API calls."
- "Grew organic blog traffic by 50,000 monthly visitors by launching an SEO keyword strategy and publishing 12 optimized articles."

Rules:
- **Lead with the result.** Start with a strong action verb that names the outcome.
- **One line each**, scannable in a few seconds.
- **Honest scope when there's no hard number**: frequency, team size, number of customers, timeline, budget, operational scope. Never guess a figure.
- **Map bullets to the 10 qualifications.** Every target qualification marked Strong or Partial should be visibly covered somewhere. Reorder bullets within each role so the most relevant come first.
- **Use the posting's vocabulary** where it truthfully describes the user's work, so keyword screens match. Don't adopt a term the user can't speak to in an interview.
- **Only use what's in the materials or what the user told you.** No invented tools, titles, metrics, or certifications.

For former founders and multi-hat operators: pick the hats this role cares about and lead with those. A founder title can read as "too senior" or "won't take direction" to some screens; let the bullets show concrete operating work.

## Step 4: The rest of the resume

- **Summary** (2 to 3 lines): who they are in the role's terms, plus their single strongest proof point.
- **Skills**: the tools and methods from the posting that the user actually has.
- Keep job titles, companies, locations, and dates exactly as in the base resume.
- Prefer projects led, cost savings, efficiency gains, awards, certifications, and continuing education when they map to the role.

## Step 5: Cover letter

Three or four short paragraphs: why this company and role specifically (from the posting or company, not generic praise), the two or three proof points that answer the role's biggest needs, how they'd handle the most obvious gap, and a direct close. Match the voice of past cover letters in `materials/` if there are any. No clichés, no em dashes.

## Step 6: Suggestions list

Give the user a short list of improvements beyond this application: weak verbs, buried results, sentence structure, skills that are under-sold for the roles they're targeting, sections to add (certifications, awards, press), and missing metrics worth digging up. Be specific and honest.

## Delivery

Use the format recorded in `job-profile.md` (Google Doc, Word, or Markdown/PDF). Save to `applications/<company>-<role>/` with clear names, e.g. `Firstname_Lastname_Resume_Company_Role`. If the role is in the tracker, tell the user they can paste the link into the "Link to Resume" column; don't write it yourself unless they ask.
