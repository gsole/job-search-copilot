# Scoring a role (Fit Score 1 to 10)

Score against the user's `job-profile.md`. Read the posting itself, not the search-result card: cards routinely mislabel location, level, and remote status.

## Order of operations

The order matters. Doing it out of order is how a role that should have been excluded ends up a 9.

1. **Hard filters.** Location, comp floor, travel, work authorization, avoided industries, and every line under "Hard filters" in the profile. If one fires, the role is out. Record which one.
2. **Red flags, with the two tests below** (OR-branch, preferred vs required). If one genuinely fires, the role is out.
3. **Caps.** Any rule that says "caps at N" (a demoted role family, the mandate test, a headcount band). Apply caps *before* adding bonuses, so a capped role can't climb back over the cap on company quality or comp.
4. **Base score** from the bands below.
5. **Adjustments** from the profile's Scoring adjustments section, then clip to any cap.
6. **Before you finalize any score below the shortlist threshold, name the single rule that held it there and re-read the posting's exact wording to confirm the rule really fires.** Most bad scores come from one rule fired on a misread.

## Base bands

- **9 to 10:** Hits target role, level, mandate type, company profile, location, and comp, with a real Tier 1 or differentiator match. 10 is rare.
- **7 to 8:** Meets most criteria with one or two minor gaps (adjacent industry, remote-only, reporting line one step off).
- **6:** Partial match, or a role held at 6 by a cap. Name the cap in the reasoning.
- **5 and below:** Not a fit. Don't log (unless the user sent it in manually).

## Reusable fit tests

These came out of real scoring mistakes. Apply whichever the profile calls for.

**The mandate test: build vs run-at-volume.** The most common near-miss is a role that reads like a builder role because it says "scalable", "repeatable", "process", but the job is throughput on something that already exists. Ask: *what exists on day one?* If the machine is already running and the job is to make it run better, it's a run role.
- Run tells: volume targets ("onboard 300+ customers monthly"), leading managers who lead specialists, offshore or BPO capacity, SLA/CSAT/NRR as the main scorecard, verbs that presuppose the thing exists ("enhance", "strengthen", "take ownership of our existing").
- Build tells: "stand up", "build the org structure, coverage model and career paths", post-merger unification across teams, designing a system of record from scratch, first hire into the function.
- When a role genuinely splits, judge which is the bigger hat and say so.

**The OR-branch test.** Postings often list several acceptable backgrounds joined by "or": "10+ years in software engineering, engineering operations, product operations, or R&D leadership." A background requirement only excludes when *every* branch is one the user lacks. If any branch matches, the red flag does not fire.

**Preferred vs required.** Only a must-have excludes. "Nice to have", "a plus", or one bullet in a long list is a gap to note in the reasoning, not an exclusion.

**The supporting-role tell.** "You are not expected to own every area", "your work will flex with the needs of the business", "support selected initiatives", "you will not lead this motion" describe a seat that assists an owner. For candidates who want ownership, this is a miss regardless of years asked for.

**Seniority scoping.** A stated range that tops out at or below the user's level ("3 to 5 years", "5 to 7 years" for someone with 10) means the seat is scoped below them, however good the language reads.

**Quota tell.** A personal bookings, expansion, renewal, or partner-sourced number, "own the number", or an OTE where variable is a large share of total. Influencing or reporting on revenue is not carrying quota.

**Business-model check for services firms and recruiters.** For a consultancy, agency, staffing firm, or "unnamed client" listing, find out what the employer actually sells before getting attached to the mandate. Great work at the wrong kind of company is still the wrong job.

## Comp rules

- Check whether a range is base or OTE. A $165K to $225K OTE on a sales-flavored role implies a base well under it.
- When a range spans multiple levels ("reflects the variety of levels we may hire at"), judge the midpoint.
- A midpoint exactly on the floor clears, but call it marginal.
- Unlisted comp: follow the profile's rule, and write "not listed" rather than assuming.

## Location traps

- "Remote, but within commuting distance of our NY/SF hubs" is on-site outside the user's city if their city isn't a hub.
- A posting that lists "Remote" among several cities but sets Location Type to Hybrid is hybrid.
- Benefits that imply attendance (housing bonus for living near the office, relocation package, daily meals on site) mean in-person.
- A remote-primary role whose headquarters happens to be in an excluded town is still remote. The rule is about the work arrangement, not the HQ address.
- If the posting is silent, write "work arrangement not stated" instead of assuming remote.

## Fit reasoning (1 to 2 sentences, goes in the tracker)

Say what drove the score: mandate type, the strongest match to a differentiator, and the biggest gap. If a cap or exemption applied, name it ("Capped at 6 by run-at-volume test"). For a senior IC seat with no direct reports, start with "IC, no team." so the level is visible. Descriptive, not a demerit.

## Manually sent roles

When the user sends a specific posting, score it honestly against the full profile. If it lands below threshold and they still want it logged, log it with a reasoning prefix like "MANUALLY ADDED [date], below threshold" so a later review doesn't mistake it for a scoring bug. If the company is strong but the role is wrong, say that, and suggest watching the company for a better-scoped opening.
